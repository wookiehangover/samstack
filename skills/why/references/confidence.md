# Evidence and confidence

| Level | Standard | How to report |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | A contemporary author explicitly states a rationale. | Attribute and cite what the author said; distinguish intent from successful outcome. |
| Supported | Independent indirect records converge on a conclusion. | Name the evidence and the reasoning step; this is derived, not a quote. |
| Inferred | Context supports a reasonable interpretation without explicit confirmation. | State the inference chain and alternatives; use calibrated language. |
| Speculative | Several explanations fit thin evidence. | Present as a possibility and name a discriminating observation. |
| Unknown | Searched records do not establish the answer, or needed records are unavailable. | State queries, coverage/time limits, and the unanswered question. |

Code behavior does not establish author intent. A label such as “performance” is weaker than a design discussion explaining the tradeoff. A stated intention does not prove the change achieved it. Later recollections can help but should be dated and compared with contemporary sources.

Preserve contradictory sources and their dates. Do not pick the newer or tidier one automatically. Treat a leading question as a hypothesis to test. Avoid turning missing history, telemetry retention, or inaccessible conversations into evidence that something never happened.

Before returning, attach evidence to each factual rationale claim or lower its confidence. Keep meaningful gaps visible. Never create an artificial gap if the available evidence fully answers the narrow question.
